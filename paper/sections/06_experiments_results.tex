\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate four frontier and regional models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SEA-LION-7B-Instruct}~\cite{aisingapore2024sealion}: The premier open-weights model pretrained on Southeast Asian corpora.
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct}: State-of-the-art open multilingual foundation model.
    \item \textbf{Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}: Global open baseline.
    \item \textbf{GPT-4o}: Closed proprietary ceiling baseline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Over-Privilege Rate ($OPR$):} Percentage of queries where the dispatched tool's privilege strictly exceeds the minimal sufficient tool ($\text{priv}(t) \sqsupset \text{priv}(t^*)$).
    \item \textbf{Task Completion Rate ($TCR$):} Percentage of queries where the user task is successfully fulfilled without permission violations.
    \item \textbf{Latency Overhead ($\Delta t$):} Additional execution time introduced by the verifier middleware (in milliseconds).
\end{itemize}

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, baseline models demonstrate a substantial surge in over-privilege as the prompt transitions from English ($24.1\% - 28.4\%$) to Indonesian ($36.8\% - 42.6\%$) and Javanese ($48.5\% - 58.0\%$). In Javanese, more than half of tool invocations are over-privileged. 

When our deterministic verifier is enabled, $OPR$ drops to **0.0\%** across all language pairs, while $TCR$ simultaneously increases (up to $+25\%$) because over-privileged calls that would otherwise fail or violate security constraints are safely redirected to valid minimal tools.

\subsection{Robustness Under Transient Faults}
When transient HTTP 504 errors are injected into minimal tools, unconstrained baseline models escalate to administrative tools in $68.4\%$ of Javanese tasks. Under our fault quarantine middleware, unauthorized privilege escalation is completely blocked ($0.0\%$), and safe backoff retry sequences are initiated.

\subsection{Runtime Overhead}
Across 1,200 evaluated trajectories, our parameter inspector and lattice traversal incurred a mean latency overhead of $\Delta t = 11.4 \pm 2.1\text{ms}$, well below the acceptable 50ms latency budget for interactive agent systems.
